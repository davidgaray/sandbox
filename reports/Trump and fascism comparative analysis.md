# Measuring Trump Against the Fascist Playbook

Donald Trump's record in office matches a large share of the *methods* that interwar fascist dictators used against democracy. It does not match the *regime outcomes* those dictators reached. The overlaps are clearest in five places: attempts to overturn an election, use of emergency and wartime powers for peacetime ends, public demands to prosecute named opponents, deployment of federal forces into opposition-run cities, and sustained rhetoric that casts the press and political rivals as enemies of the nation. Mussolini, Hitler and Franco went on to abolish elections, ban opposition parties, destroy independent unions, impose prior censorship and fold party militias into the state. None of that has happened in the United States as of October 2026. Courts have struck down many of Trump's signature measures, the 2020 transfer of power took place, the November 2026 midterms are going ahead, and a protest of roughly 8 million people in March 2026 was not repressed. Scholars are split on what to call this. Historians who define fascism by behavior and trajectory, led by Robert Paxton, now accept the label. Those who define it as a revolutionary ideology carried by a mass paramilitary party (Roger Griffin, Stanley Payne, Richard Evans) reject it. Most empirical political scientists use "competitive authoritarianism" or "patrimonialism" instead. Every major democracy index recorded a sharp U.S. decline in 2025, and none classifies the country as fascist or as a closed autocracy. Readers should therefore treat this as a story of serious democratic erosion that uses a recognizable authoritarian toolkit, with the fascist label being a definitional dispute rather than a settled finding.

## Interwar dictators dismantled democracy through legal channels first

The historical baseline matters because most popular comparisons collapse three quite different regimes into one. **Mussolini and Hitler both came to power legally**: a head of state appointed each of them under pressure from street violence. Each then took democracy apart in a recognizable sequence. In Italy, Blackshirt squads "often financed by landowners or industrialists" smashed union offices in 1920–22. King Victor Emmanuel III refused to declare martial law against the March on Rome and appointed Mussolini prime minister of a coalition government in October 1922 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Italy/The-Fascist-era)). The 1923 Acerbo law gave any bloc winning as little as 25% of the vote an absolute majority, and the Fascist list took 64% in the intimidated 1924 election ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Italy/The-Fascist-era)). Socialist deputy Giacomo Matteotti challenged the results and was murdered by Fascist squadristi. The King refused to dismiss Mussolini, and by 1926 the *leggi fascistissime* had made Italy a one-party state and abolished local elections ([Wikipedia: Matteotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Matteotti); [Wikipedia: Italian fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism)).

Hitler moved faster, taking about 18 months against Mussolini's four years. Hindenburg appointed him chancellor on 30 January 1933, after the Nazi vote had *fallen* from 37% to 33%, by elites who "felt confident that they could control Hitler" ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nazi-rise-to-power)). Within a month the Reichstag Fire Decree had suspended freedom of speech, press and assembly and allowed arrest without charge, becoming "a permanent feature of the Nazi police state" ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/reichstag-fire-decree)). The Enabling Act of March 1933 let Hitler legislate without parliament ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nazi-rise-to-power)). Unions were seized on 2 May, the SPD was banned in June, and the July 1933 law made the NSDAP the only legal party ([Wikipedia: Gleichschaltung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleichschaltung)). In August 1934 the armed forces swore "unconditional obedience" to Hitler personally ([Wikipedia: Hitler Oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler_Oath)). **Franco is the outlier.** He took power through a military coup and civil war, then made repression legal after the fact with the retroactive 1939 Law of Political Responsibilities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Francisco-Franco); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Political_Responsibilities)). Paul Preston describes a postwar "machinery of trials, executions, prisons and concentration camps" ([ALBA/Preston](https://albavolunteer.org/2011/09/paul-preston-on-the-spanish-holocaust/)).

All three regimes shared a set of methods: a single party, leader titles (*Duce*, *Führer*, *Caudillo*), prior censorship, abolition of independent labor, legal penalties for political opponents, nationalist rebirth myths and traditionalist family policy. Germany alone made exterminatory racial antisemitism state doctrine. The Nuremberg Laws restricted citizenship to those "of German or related blood," and USHMM notes that propaganda "created an atmosphere tolerant of violence against Jews" *before* the laws were passed ([USHMM Nuremberg Laws](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nuremberg-race-laws); [USHMM Propaganda](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-propaganda)). That sequence, rhetoric first and law after, is one reason scholars take rhetoric seriously. The most important structural lesson is that **the decisive gatekeepers were heads of state, armies and business elites, not voters**. Industrialists pledged roughly 2.07 million Reichsmarks at the secret meeting of 20 February 1933, where Göring promised the coming election would "surely be the last one for the next ten years" ([Wikipedia: Secret Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Meeting_of_20_February_1933)).

Scholarly frameworks turn these cases into criteria, but they fall into two families that answer different questions. **Fascism frameworks** define a specific phenomenon. Paxton calls fascism "a form of political behavior marked by obsessive preoccupation with community decline, humiliation, or victimhood," pursued by "a mass-based party of committed nationalist militants, working in uneasy but effective collaboration with traditional elites," that "abandons democratic liberties and pursues with redemptive violence and without legal or ethical restraints goals of internal cleansing and external expansion" ([Wikipedia: Definitions of fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitions_of_fascism)). Griffin requires a "palingenetic form of populist ultra-nationalism," meaning a revolutionary myth of national rebirth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palingenetic_ultranationalism)). Payne lists ideology, the fascist "negations" (including anti-conservatism) and a militarized mass style ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitions_of_fascism)). Eco's fourteen "Ur-Fascism" traits come with the claim that "it is enough that one of them be present to allow fascism to coagulate around it" ([NYRB](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1995/06/22/ur-fascism/)). That threshold makes his list easy to apply and prone to false positives. **Authoritarianism frameworks** describe how elected leaders erode democracy and do not define fascism at all. Levitsky and Ziblatt's four indicators are the most usable checklist: rejecting democratic rules, denying opponents' legitimacy, tolerating violence, and curtailing civil liberties ([Table, Levitsky & Ziblatt 2018](https://journals.umcs.pl/al/article/downloadSuppFile/12210/6186)). Nancy Bermeo shows that modern backsliding works mainly through "executive aggrandizement" and "strategic electoral manipulation" rather than coups ([Journal of Democracy](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/on-democratic-backsliding/)). The distinction matters here: a leader can score high on the behavioral indicators and still fail the structural tests for fascism (mass militant party, revolutionary new order, expansionism).

### The 2020 election and its sequel are the closest functional parallel

The interwar regimes turned elections into rituals: the Acerbo majority bonus, the intimidated March 1933 vote, local elections abolished in 1926. Trump's conduct after November 2020 is the strongest documented attempt in modern U.S. history to keep power despite an election result, and it fits several Levitsky–Ziblatt indicators directly. On the recorded 2 January 2021 call, he told Georgia's secretary of state, "I just want to find 11,780 votes, which is one more than we have" ([Election Law Blog](https://electionlawblog.org/2021/i-just-want-to-find-11780-votes-in-extraordinary-hour-long-call-trump-pressures-georgia-secretary-of-state-to-recalculate-the-vote-in-his-favor/)). The House January 6 Committee's 800-page report documented the fake-elector scheme, the pressure on Pence (a chapter titled after a judge's phrase, "a coup in search of a legal theory"), and "187 minutes of dereliction" during the Capitol attack, and it made four criminal referrals ([NPR via KAXE](https://www.kaxe.org/2022-12-23/the-jan-6-committees-final-report-details-criminal-referrals-against-trump)).

The outcomes diverge sharply from the historical cases. Trump and his allies lost **more than 60 post-election lawsuits** ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2021/jan/08/joe-biden/joe-biden-right-more-60-trumps-election-lawsuits-l/)). The Supreme Court dismissed *Texas v. Pennsylvania* ([SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/case-files/cases/texas-v-pennsylvania/)). Pence wrote that his oath "constrains me from claiming unilateral authority to determine which electoral votes should be counted" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/read-pences-full-letter-saying-he-cant-claim-unilateral-authority-to-reject-electoral-votes)). Attorney General Barr said publicly there was no "fraud on a scale that could have affected a different outcome" ([AP via ADN](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2020/12/01/no-evidence-of-widespread-election-fraud-that-would-change-election-result-attorney-general-says/)). Power changed hands on 20 January 2021. Accountability also failed. The Senate acquitted 57–43, with seven Republicans voting to convict ([Michigan Advance](https://michiganadvance.com/2021/02/14/senate-acquits-trump-in-his-second-impeachment-trial-7-republicans-vote-to-convict/)). The federal case was dismissed after Trump's 2024 election ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/11/25/nx-s1-5205376/jan-6-trump-case)), and the Georgia case was dropped in November 2025 ([NPR via Wyoming Public Media](https://www.wyomingpublicmedia.org/2025-11-26/the-georgia-election-interference-case-against-trump-and-others-has-been-dropped)).

In the second term the pattern moved from rhetoric toward state action. In early February 2026 Trump said, "The Republicans ought to nationalize the voting," in "at least — many — 15 places" ([Democracy Now](https://www.democracynow.org/2026/2/3/headlines/fulton_county_to_sue_over_fbi_ballot_seizures_as_trump_says_gop_should_nationalize_elections); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/04/trump-nationalize-election-2020-fraud-claims)). This came days after federal investigators seized **more than 600 boxes of Fulton County's 2020 ballots and records** ([Axios Atlanta](https://www.axios.com/local/atlanta/2026/02/05/fulton-county-election-raid-trump-retribution-georgia-voting)). Courts permanently enjoined core parts of his March 2025 elections executive order, including in a June 2026 ruling, and *Time* summarized the result as "Trump Is Trying to Nationalize Elections. States Won't Let Him" ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/04/02/trump-is-trying-to-nationalize-elections-states-won-t-let-him/)). Trump floated a third term in 2025, then said "I guess I'm not allowed to run… It's too bad" ([EWN/AFP](https://www.ewn.co.za/2025/10/29/trump-said-not-allowed-to-run-for-third-term-too-bad)). Elections have been contested but not abolished, which is why this report rates the parallel **partial**: the intent and early steps resemble the historical method, but the decisive act of cancelling or rigging the vote has not occurred.

### Emergency and wartime statutes stretched, then trimmed by courts

The Reichstag Fire Decree is the archetype of an emergency used to suspend rights. Snyder calls this "the oldest trick in the Hitlerian book" ([search summary of Snyder](https://new-ideal.aynrand.org/p/crisis-lies-morality-snyders-lessons-on-tyranny)). Trump's emergency use is different in kind: it reallocates money and trade authority rather than suspending civil liberties. It does, however, repeatedly turn crisis statutes to peacetime ends. In 2019 he declared a border emergency to fund a wall after Congress refused. Congress voted to end the emergency with twelve Republican senators joining Democrats, and his veto survived ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2019/03/26/706843365/trumps-national-emergency-stands-as-house-fails-to-override-veto)). In the second term he imposed worldwide tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in *Learning Resources v. Trump* (February 2026) that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/scotus-rejects-ieepa-tariffs-refund-opportunities-and-uncertainty-emerge)). The administration complied and moved within days to Section 122 of the Trade Act, which caps tariffs at 15% and lapses after 150 days unless Congress extends them ([WVTM13/Hearst](https://www.wvtm13.com/article/how-trump-is-imposing-new-tariffs-supreme-courts/70450669)). He invoked the 1798 Alien Enemies Act against alleged gang members as a "predatory incursion." A Fifth Circuit panel rejected that premise, but the en banc court dismissed the case as moot in August 2026 after the government deported the plaintiffs under other statutes, leaving the legal question unresolved ([Volokh/Reason](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/08/13/en-banc-fifth-circuit-dismisses-alien-enemies-act-case-as-moot/)). The common thread is aggressive assertion followed by compliance with workarounds. This report rates it **partial**: the method echoes the Weimar pattern of emergency governance, but the 1933 step of suspending rights by decree has no analogue.

### Political violence: encouragement and pardons, but no party militia

The squadristi and the SA, which grew to 4 million members by 1934, were the engine of fascist takeovers ([USHMM SA](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-sa)). Trump's record matches Levitsky and Ziblatt's third indicator, tolerating or encouraging violence, but not the organizational model. As a candidate he told a 2016 Iowa crowd to "knock the crap out of" would-be tomato-throwers and promised to "pay for the legal fees" ([TIME](https://time.com/4203094/donald-trump-hecklers/)). At the September 2020 debate, asked to condemn white supremacists, he told the Proud Boys to "stand back and stand by," and the group adopted the phrase as a slogan ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/30/proud-boys-celebrate-after-trump-debate-mention.html)). The January 6 Committee documented the roles of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers in the Capitol attack and their links to Trump allies ([NPR via KAXE](https://www.kaxe.org/2022-12-23/the-jan-6-committees-final-report-details-criminal-referrals-against-trump)). On his first day back in office he pardoned or commuted the sentences of roughly 1,500 January 6 defendants (reported; not re-verified for this report) ([White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/granting-pardons-and-commutation-of-sentences-for-certain-offenses-relating-to-the-events-at-or-near-the-united-states-capitol-on-january-6-2021/)). The 2017 Charlottesville remarks remain disputed. Trump said there were "very fine people, on both sides" and, in the same press conference, that neo-Nazis "should be condemned totally" ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2019/04/26/context-trumps-very-fine-people-both-sides-remarks/)).

Paxton points to this cluster as the decisive change. He wrote in January 2021 that "open encouragement of civic violence to overturn an election crosses a red line" ([summary of Newsweek op-ed](https://thewayofimprovement.blog/2024/10/24/a-historian-of-fascism-changes-his-mind-yes-trump-is-a-fascist/)), and said January 6 reminded him of Mussolini's Blackshirts and the February 1934 Paris riots ([Columbia History](https://history.columbia.edu/2024/10/24/robert-paxton-was-interviewed-by-the-new-york-times-on-trumps-relation-to-fascism-and-how-january-6th-inspired-a-reconsideration-of-whether-or-not-trump-could-be-considered-a-fascist/)). Critics answer that there is no party militia under the leader's command, no sustained campaign of street terror, and nothing like the SA's Köpenick "blood-week," in which 91 people died ([USHMM SA](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-sa)). This report rates the parallel **partial**: the leader-level signals match, while the organizational infrastructure is missing.

### Federal force in opposition cities is the sharpest second-term escalation

Historically, once fascists held power, violence moved from party militias into state organs such as the SS and the Vichy Milice ([USHMM SA](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-sa); [Wikipedia: Vichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France)). The comparable U.S. question is whether federal coercive forces are being turned against domestic political opponents. The first term produced signals that were largely checked. At Lafayette Square in June 2020, largely peaceful protesters were cleared with chemical irritants before a presidential photo opportunity. General Milley later apologized: "I should not have been there" ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/i-should-not-have-been-there-milley-apologizes-role-trump-n1229876)). Defense Secretary Esper publicly opposed invoking the Insurrection Act and later wrote that Trump asked, "Can't you just shoot them? Just shoot them in the legs or something?" ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2022/05/02/mark-esper-book-trump-protesters)). In Portland, DHS confirmed that camouflaged federal officers detained protesters using unmarked vehicles ([NPR via KUNC](https://www.kunc.org/npr-news/2020-07-17/federal-officers-use-unmarked-vehicles-to-grab-people-in-portland-dhs-confirms)).

The second term went further. Trump told about 800 generals and admirals in September 2025 that American cities could be "training grounds for our military" and that "America is under invasion from within… No different than a foreign enemy" ([Wikipedia compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_military_deployments_by_the_second_Trump_administration)). About 4,000 federalized Guard troops and 700 Marines went to Los Angeles over the governor's objection. Judge Charles Breyer found the deployment violated the Posse Comitatus Act, with "no rebellion, nor was civilian law enforcement unable to respond," and troops withdrew in December 2025 ([Wikipedia: Newsom v. Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsom_v._Trump)). The Supreme Court held 6–3 in *Trump v. Illinois* that Trump likely lacked authority to federalize the Guard for Chicago ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/128371/trump-v-illinois-supreme-court/)). The Washington, D.C. deployment continues through the end of 2026 after an appeals court allowed it ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/appeals-court-says-trumps-national-guard-deployment-in-washington-can-continue-for-now)). The gravest episode was **Operation Metro Surge in Minneapolis–St. Paul**, where more than 3,000 federal immigration agents operated and **two U.S.-citizen protesters, Renée Good and Alex Pretti, were killed by federal agents** in January 2026 ([Wikipedia: Operation Metro Surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Metro_Surge); [WTOP/AP](https://wtop.com/national/2026/02/a-timeline-of-the-trump-administrations-immigration-crackdown-in-minnesota/)). Trump threatened the Insurrection Act and 1,500 active-duty soldiers were put on standby, but he did not invoke it, and Northern Command stood down in February ([Wikipedia compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_military_deployments_by_the_second_Trump_administration)). Defenders frame the deployments as responses to crime and to threats against federal officers, and point to the 4,000 arrests Tom Homan claimed in Minneapolis ([Wikipedia: Metro Surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Metro_Surge)). This report rates the parallel **partial to strong**. The rhetoric of an internal enemy combined with troops sent to opposition-governed cities is the closest match to the historical shift toward state violence, while court limits, the withdrawals and the non-invocation of the Insurrection Act mark the difference.

### The press faces pressure and prosecution, not prior censorship

Fascist regimes used decree and ministry to control the press: Goebbels's ministry coordinated "art, music, theater, films, books, radio, educational materials, and the press" ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-propaganda)), and Franco imposed prior censorship in 1938 ([Wikipedia: Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain)). Trump's first-term press rhetoric was intense: the media is "the enemy of the American People" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2017/2/18/trump-declares-media-the-enemy-of-the-american-people)), and network "licenses must be challenged and, if appropriate, revoked" ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/series-tweets-trump-renews-feud-nbc-raises-broadcaster-license-issue)). No licenses were revoked. In the second term, rhetoric became regulatory and prosecutorial leverage. Poynter logged **more than 100 federal actions** against the press and more than 15 media lawsuits. The AP was barred from presidential spaces for refusing to use "Gulf of America." DOJ guidelines were loosened to allow subpoenas of journalists, the FBI searched a *Washington Post* reporter's home, journalists Don Lemon and Georgia Fort were arrested covering a protest, and Congress rescinded about $1 billion from public broadcasting ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2026/trump-press-freedom/)). FCC chair Brendan Carr warned broadcasters to "correct course before their license renewal comes up." The FCC ordered eight ABC stations to file renewals years early, and Disney sued; the case was argued around 6 October 2026 ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/06/disney-abc-battle-trump-s-fcc-in-court-over-free-speech-protections/)). Courts have mostly sided with the press. A judge ruled for the *New York Times* against Pentagon escort rules ([Mother Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/03/trump-new-york-times-pentagon-press-credentials-lawsuit-judge-ruling-defense-department-pete-hegeseth/)), and CNN's media analyst concluded that "America's courts keep defending the First Amendment" ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/25/media/trump-free-speech-media-first-amendment-stelter-analysis)). V-Dem nonetheless reports U.S. freedom of expression "at its lowest level since the end of WWII" ([V-Dem 2026](https://www.uexternado.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/V-Dem_Institute_Democracy_Report_2026_lowres.pdf)). This report rates the parallel **partial**: it resembles competitive-authoritarian harassment (regulatory pressure, litigation, access control) rather than fascist state monopoly.

### Prosecuting enemies: presidential demands met grand-jury and judicial resistance

The Reichstag Fire Decree allowed opponents to be jailed without charge, and Franco's 1939 law criminalized past loyalty to the Republic ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/reichstag-fire-decree); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Political_Responsibilities)). Trump's first term showed pressure on law enforcement for personal ends. James Comey testified under oath that Trump said, "I need loyalty, I expect loyalty," and asked him to "let this go" regarding Michael Flynn ([Comey statement](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/documents/os-jcomey-060817.pdf)). Mueller examined about ten possible obstruction episodes, including Trump's direction to have Mueller removed, which White House Counsel McGahn refused, and wrote that the report "does not exonerate him" ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2019/jul/26/politifacts-mueller-report-book-club-volume-2)). Clemency for allies followed: Roger Stone's commutation and Paul Manafort's pardon ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-07-11/trump-will-commute-roger-stone-s-prison-sentence); [Anadolu](https://aa.com.tr/en/americas/trump-announces-new-round-of-pardons/2086922)).

The second term moved from obstruction to prosecution. On 20 September 2025 Trump publicly pressed his attorney general to "prosecute" Comey and others. The interim U.S. attorney, who had reportedly advised against charges, resigned. His replacement was sworn in on 22 September, and Comey was indicted three days later ([Wikipedia: Prosecution of James Comey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecution_of_James_Comey)). New York Attorney General Letitia James was indicted in October. A federal judge dismissed both indictments in November 2025 because the prosecutor had been unlawfully appointed, and **two grand juries then declined to re-indict James** ([Wikipedia: Prosecution of Letitia James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecution_of_Letitia_James)). Comey was indicted again in North Carolina in April 2026 on a threats charge ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/comey-appears-in-court-in-trump-threat-case-thats-likely-to-pose-a-challenge-for-justice-department)). John Bolton, whose classified-documents case was viewed inside DOJ as stronger, agreed in June 2026 to plead guilty ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/04/bolton-guilty-plea-trump-national-security-advisor.html)). The Fourth Circuit appeal of the Comey and James dismissals was argued on 15 September 2026 and is pending. This report rates presidential intent **partial to strong**, since the president publicly demanded named prosecutions and they followed within days. It rates outcomes **weak**, because judges and citizen grand juries have blocked most of these cases.

### Civil service: loyalty over neutrality, but without the Aryan paragraph

The Nazi civil service law of April 1933 removed officials "not of Aryan descent" and anyone whose record did not guarantee "unconditional support to the national state" ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/law-for-the-restoration-of-the-professional-civil-service)). Trump's version targets political neutrality, not ethnicity. In about six weeks in 2020 he removed five inspectors general, including the official who handled the Ukraine whistleblower complaint ([PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2020/may/19/trump-has-pushed-out-5-inspectors-general-april-he/)). Executive Order 13957 created "Schedule F" to strip removal protections from policy-related posts. OPM approved moving 136 positions, but no placements were completed before Biden revoked it ([GAO-22-105504](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-22-105504.pdf)). The second term revived Schedule F, fired roughly 17 inspectors general without statutory notice, and ran mass firings through DOGE (all reported; not re-verified for this report) ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/28/2025-01762/restoring-accountability-to-policy-influencing-positions-within-the-federal-workforce); [Wikipedia: IG dismissals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_dismissals_of_inspectors_general)). The Supreme Court then **overruled *Humphrey's Executor*** in *Trump v. Slaughter* (June 2026), upholding his removal of FTC commissioners over policy disagreements ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/06/supreme-court-overrules-humphreys-executor)). Interpretations of that ruling diverge. Heritage's *Daily Signal* headlined that the Constitution "Allows the President to Be President" ([Daily Signal](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/06/29/supreme-court-trump-v-slaughter/)), while 90% of political scientists surveyed by Bright Line Watch rated the ruling a threat to democracy ([Bright Line Watch](https://brightlinewatch.org/a-partial-democratic-rebound/)). This report rates the parallel **partial**. The shift toward loyalty-based administration is real and now has judicial backing, but it has no ethnic or ideological purge criterion and no personal oath.

### Scapegoating: harsh rhetoric, contested policy, no racial citizenship regime

Trump's language about immigrants and opponents is the area where commentators most often invoke the 1930s. As a candidate he called for "a total and complete shutdown of Muslims entering the United States" ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2018/06/supreme-court-upholds-travel-ban)). In a 2018 meeting he asked about immigrants from "shithole countries," a phrase he confirmed using in 2025 ([Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/news/dick-durbin-confirms-trump-shithole-comments/); [CNN via KVIA](https://kvia.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2025/12/10/almost-eight-years-later-trump-confirms-he-used-the-phrase-shithole-countries/)). In 2023 he vowed to "root out" opponents who "live like vermin" and said immigrants were "poisoning the blood of our country," remarks the ADL and historians criticized ([Boston Globe/WaPo](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/11/13/nation/trump-calls-political-enemies-vermin-echoing-dictators-hitler-mussolini)). Enacted policy produced mixed results in court. The third travel-ban version was upheld 5–4, over Justice Sotomayor's dissent that it was "motivated by anti-Muslim animus" ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2018/06/supreme-court-upholds-travel-ban)). Family separation was halted by a class-wide injunction ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10180.html)). The birthright-citizenship order, the closest analogue to a citizenship restriction, was struck down 6–3 in *Trump v. Barbara* (June 2026) ([CRS LSB11468](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB11468.html)). This report rates the parallel **partial**. The rhetoric closely tracks Paxton's and Eco's markers (an endangered community, a dangerous out-group), and enforcement has been severe. The defining Nazi steps (racial citizenship law, intermarriage bans, extermination) have no counterpart, and the one attempt to redefine citizenship by birth failed in court.

### Leader cult, falsehood, culture and expansion

The personalist marks are visible but thin compared with the interwar cults. "I alone can fix it" ([Politico transcript](https://www.politico.com/story/2016/07/full-transcript-donald-trump-nomination-acceptance-speech-at-rnc-225974)) and the loyalty demand to Comey echo Paxton's sixth "mobilizing passion," the national chief "alone capable of incarnating the group's destiny" ([Gardner, quoting Paxton](https://dgardner.substack.com/p/is-it-fascism)). Trump's praise of autocrats meets a Levitsky–Ziblatt sub-indicator. He said Kim Jong Un "wrote me beautiful letters… We fell in love," and remarked of Xi's lifetime tenure, "Maybe we'll have to give that a shot some day" ([CNN via KPAX](https://www.kpax.com/cnn-us-politics/2019/07/02/15-times-donald-trump-praised-authoritarian-rulers/)). There was no personal oath like that of 1934, and senior officials refused orders. On truth, the *Washington Post* counted **30,573 false or misleading claims** in the first term, rising from about 6 to 39 a day ([summary of WaPo](https://www.yahoo.com/now/trump-made-30-573-false-205400099.html)). The stolen-election claim became the basis for the 2020 pressure campaign. Hitler coined the term "big lie" in *Mein Kampf*, and Jeffrey Herf argues the Nazis themselves practiced it ([Wikipedia: Big lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_lie)). Volume of falsehood is a **strong** parallel on Stanley's "unreality" pillar. A state propaganda monopoly is absent: the claims were contested by Trump's own attorney general, by courts and by a free press.

On universities, the administration cut more than $2.6 billion from Harvard. Judge Allison Burroughs ruled the cuts unconstitutional, calling antisemitism "a smokescreen for a targeted, ideologically-motivated assault on this country's premier universities." The appeal is pending ([ABC/AP](https://abc7.com/post/president-donald-trumps-administration-will-appeal-judges-order-reversing-federal-funding-cuts-harvard-university/18300843/)). Three Yale scholars of authoritarianism, Jason Stanley, Timothy Snyder and Marci Shore, left for Toronto in 2025 ([CNN via KVIA](https://kvia.com/cnn-national/2025/03/28/3-ivy-league-scholars-plan-to-leave-us-and-teach-in-canada-amid-trump-administrations-higher-education-battle/)). Book burnings, mass faculty dismissals and a ministry of culture have no counterpart, so this is **partial**. On economics, the corporatist abolition of independent unions under Mussolini and Hitler ([Wikipedia: Economy of Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Fascist_Italy)) has no U.S. analogue. A government equity stake in Intel and selective tariff exemptions (reported) fit Ben-Ghiat's "corruption" and Hanson and Kopstein's patrimonialism better than corporatism, so this is **weak**.

Expansionism is where the second term most complicates an earlier argument against the label. Payne had cited Trump's "noninterventionist foreign military policy" as a reason he was not fascist ([HNN](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/why-trump-isnt-a-fascist)), and Evans pointed to the absence of any "hunger for conquest" ([New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/long-reads/2021/01/why-trump-isnt-fascist)). Since then, U.S. boat strikes killed at least 117 people by January 2026. On 3 January 2026 U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in an operation for which, AP noted, "the legal authority… was not immediately clear" ([AP via CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=1054535)). A war with Iran began in late February 2026 (not examined in detail in this research) ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2026/trump-press-freedom/)). Trump threatened tariffs on eight European allies over Greenland, then withdrew them at Davos ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/world-news/trump-says-greenland-agreement-has-been-reached/)). None of this amounts to annexation or Mussolini's conquest of Ethiopia, which killed some 250,000 Ethiopians ([Wikipedia: Italian fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism)). The "noninterventionist" premise of Payne's argument, however, no longer describes the record. This report rates the parallel **partial**.

## Where the record diverges from fascist rule

The divergences are structural, and they are the ones scholars treat as decisive. First, **there is no mass party militia and no one-party state**. Payne notes that Trump "never founded a new fascist party, never embraced a coherent new revolutionary ideology" ([HNN](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/why-trump-isnt-a-fascist)). The Republican Party competes in elections, and the opposition governs states, sues the federal government and wins. Governors Newsom and Pritzker beat the administration at the Supreme Court over Guard deployments ([Wikipedia compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_military_deployments_by_the_second_Trump_administration)). Second, **elections continue and power has changed hands**: the 2018 and 2022 midterms took place, Trump left office in 2021, and the November 2026 midterms are scheduled. Third, **courts rule against the president and he formally complies**. The administration lost at the Supreme Court on tariffs, Guard federalization and birthright citizenship, and followed the rulings, though often with workarounds (Section 122 tariffs, deporting plaintiffs until a case was moot, re-indicting Comey elsewhere). The research notes infer that this pattern is closer to "malicious compliance" than either defiance or full acceptance. Fourth, **mass dissent is not repressed**. The March 2026 "No Kings" protests drew about **8 million people at roughly 3,300 locations** without mass suppression ([Wikipedia: March 2026 No Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2026_No_Kings_protests); [CSMonitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Society/2026/0329/no-kings-protest-trump)). Fifth, **federalism decentralizes elections and policing**, and the Century Foundation identifies this as a barrier showing "the right wing has not yet consolidated an authoritarian system" ([Policy Commons](https://policycommons.net/artifacts/42679870/centurys-new-democracy-meter-shows-america-took-an-authoritarian-turn-in-2025/43578927/)). Sixth, there is no exterminatory racial program. Seventh, many signature policies, including tax cuts, deregulation and judicial appointments, are conventional Republican priorities.

The historical comparison still cuts both ways on elite behavior. In Italy and Germany, the king, the president and the army leadership chose to hand power over or not to resist. In Trump's first term, his own appointees did the opposite at the decisive moments: McGahn, Esper, Barr, Raffensperger and Pence each refused. Those refusals depended on individuals rather than on automatic mechanisms, and Congress never used its removal power. The second term's institutional picture is mixed in a way the interwar cases were not. The Supreme Court expanded presidential immunity in 2024 ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-supreme-court-rules-on-presidential-immunity)) and presidential removal power in 2026, while striking down the tariffs and the birthright order. Levitsky and Ziblatt's concept of "semi-loyal democrats," politicians who tolerate or excuse anti-democratic behavior by their own side ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2023/09/scholars-warn-of-danger-in-an-outdated-constitution-democracy-tyranny-of-the-minority)), is the closest modern analogue to Paxton's elite-collaboration stage. Whether that analogy holds is an open empirical question, not a settled one.

## Scholars split over the label, and democracy indices point to hybrid regime

### The label debate turns on definitions and on political consequences

Positions track definitions closely. **Pro-label scholars use behavioral or processual definitions.** Paxton resisted the term for years, then concluded in January 2021 that "the label now seems not just acceptable but necessary," and in 2024 described Trumpism as a mass phenomenon "from below," like Italian Fascism and Nazism ([summary of Newsweek](https://thewayofimprovement.blog/2024/10/24/a-historian-of-fascism-changes-his-mind-yes-trump-is-a-fascist/); [Columbia History](https://history.columbia.edu/2024/10/24/robert-paxton-was-interviewed-by-the-new-york-times-on-trumps-relation-to-fascism-and-how-january-6th-inspired-a-reconsideration-of-whether-or-not-trump-could-be-considered-a-fascist/)). Stanley moved from analyzing "fascist politics" in 2018 to telling *Vanity Fair* in 2025 that "the federal government is a fascist regime" ([Washington Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/latest-news/yale-prof-who-got-new-job-in-canada-calls-us-fascist-leaves-for-toronto-interview/)). A 2025 open letter signed by more than 400 scholars, including about 30 Nobel laureates, warned that "the signs of authoritarianism, and its more militaristic sibling fascism, are here" ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/31-nobel-laureates-warn-signs-024116934.html)). Insider testimony carries unusual weight. John Kelly, Trump's former chief of staff, said Trump falls "into the general definition of fascist" and had said "Hitler did some good things" ([ABC News](https://abc7news.com/post/donald-trumps-former-chief-staff-says-he-fits-fascist-definition-prefers-dictator-approach/15458500/)). Thirteen former officials backed Kelly ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2024-election/13-former-trump-administration-officials-sign-open-letter-backing-john-rcna177227)), and Mark Milley reportedly told Bob Woodward that Trump is "fascist to the core," a secondhand quote ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/half-survey-view-trump-fascist-132939399.html)). Trump's campaign disputed Kelly's account. As early as 2015–16, Ross Douthat found that Trumpism showed "at least seven" of Eco's markers, while concluding Trump was only "a little bit fascistic" ([Newsweek summary](https://www.newsweek.com/donald-trump-fascist-403058)).

**Anti-label scholars split into two camps.** The ideal-type historians argue the structural criteria are not met. Griffin is reported as saying Trump "has given no sign that he intends the destruction of the U.S. constitutional system and its replacement by a totalitarian 'new order,'" and classifies him as a "radical right-wing populist" (the original venue of this quote is unconfirmed) ([Fair Observer/Betz](https://www.fairobserver.com/region/north_america/hans-georg-betz-trumpism-after-trump-capitol-hill-insurrection-fascism-american-democracy-news-10191/)). Writing just after January 6, Evans argued that Trump lacked fascism's paramilitary mass movement and drive for conquest. He also claimed a consensus of specialists against the label, but his list included Ruth Ben-Ghiat, whose own work places Trump in the strongman lineage, so that claim is contested ([New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/long-reads/2021/01/why-trump-isnt-fascist)). David Runciman called Trump "too flaky to be an actual fascist" ([Zimmer](https://thomaszimmer.substack.com/p/donald-trump-american-fascist)). The left-wing skeptics argue from consequences and regime weakness. Samuel Moyn warned that the analogy "disguises that he is quintessentially American" and entrenches attachment to failing institutions ([3 Quarks Daily repost of NYRB](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=178607)). Corey Robin called Trumpism "almost the complete opposite of fascism" because it relies on, rather than rejects, counter-majoritarian institutions such as the Electoral College, Senate and courts ([Jewish Currents](https://jewishcurrents.org/almost-the-complete-opposite-of-fascism)). Dylan Riley argued that "the reason that Trump is not a fascist is that American democracy itself is too sick to produce true fascism" ([Verso](https://www.versobooks.com/blogs/news/4189-what-is-trump)). From the political right, critics call the label partisan: only 12% of Republicans in an October 2024 ABC/Ipsos poll called Trump a fascist, against 49% of all registered voters ([AOL/The Hill](https://www.aol.com/half-survey-view-trump-fascist-132939399.html)).

**Positions have shifted in the second term, but only on one axis.** After ICE arrested green-card holder Mahmoud Khalil in March 2025, Moyn called it "a big and flagrant step towards fascism." That is not a full concession, despite how some commentators have described it ([X post](https://x.com/samuelmoyn/status/1898895799137767517)). *Dissent* reports that Robin said "I have since turned out to be wrong" (seen via snippet only) ([Dissent](https://dissentmagazine.org/article/what-was-the-fascism-debate/)). These reversals answer the left skeptics' objection that the regime was too weak to repress. They do not answer the ideal-typists' objections about the missing mass party and revolutionary ideology, and no 2025–26 restatement by Evans, Payne or Griffin was found. Douthat's 2026 farewell column called the second term corrupt, bullying and destabilizing without adopting the fascist label ([Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/trumpism-has-failed-ross-douthat-rips-trumps-corrupt-and-bullying-and-destabilizing-second-term-in-ny-times-farewell/)).

### Three alternative frameworks capture the regime mechanics more precisely

**Competitive authoritarianism** has become the dominant framework among empirical political scientists. Levitsky and Way define it as a regime in which democratic institutions remain the main route to power but "incumbents violate those rules so often and to such an extent" that minimum democratic standards are not met ([Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/17196/summary)). In February 2025 they wrote that the U.S. was "transitioning from a constitutional democracy to a form of authoritarian rule." By May 2025, writing with Ziblatt, they said it had "already become a competitive authoritarian regime." They explicitly do not call it fascism ([Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/node/1132777); [Fair Observer](https://www.fairobserver.com/politics/america-crosses-into-competitive-authoritarian-rule-under-trump/)). **Patrimonialism**, as framed by Stephen Hanson and Jeffrey Kopstein, describes a ruler who treats the state "as if it were his personal possession," handing parts of it to loyalists and family, and places Trump alongside Putin, Erdoğan and Modi ([ECPS interview](https://www.populismstudies.org/professor-hanson-trumps-patrimonial-rule-treats-the-state-as-personal-property-and-undermining-impartial-governance/)). Riley's earlier "ramshackle patrimonialism and weak charisma" anticipated this view ([Verso](https://www.versobooks.com/blogs/news/4189-what-is-trump)). **Populism** is Griffin's classification ("radical right-wing populist"), and Federico Finchelstein's historical work distinguishes "populism as a form of democracy" from "fascism as a form of dictatorship" ([UC Press description](https://www.labyrinthbooks.com/from-fascism-to-populism-in-history)). These frameworks complement Paxton's rather than contradict it. Competitive authoritarianism and patrimonialism describe *how power is exercised*, while Paxton's "mobilizing passions" describe the *emotional politics* that sustain it. A leader can show the second while governing through the first.

The indices agree on serious erosion with elections still functioning. That fits the competitive-authoritarian diagnosis and also the skeptics' point that institutions still constrain. Bright Line Watch's July 2026 regime-type question shows the expert split precisely. **55% of experts describe the U.S. as having free and fair elections with only moderate checks, 41% as having "unfair, tilted elections and weak checks,"** and 1% say elections are non-functional. Experts are 82% confident that votes will be counted as cast nationally. They forecast a fall to 51–56 by the end of 2026, and project 49 by 2032 if a Trump-endorsed Republican wins in 2028 versus 72 if a Democrat wins ([Bright Line Watch](https://brightlinewatch.org/a-partial-democratic-rebound/)). In the same survey, 11 of 37 tracked erosion events had occurred, including pardons for allies and DOJ investigations of opponents. Polity had already scored the U.S. "at the cusp of autocracy" in 2020 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/04/15/multiple-indicators-show-a-decline-in-the-health-of-americas-democracy-in-2025/)).

## Conclusion

The comparison shows that the useful question for a reader is less "is Trump a fascist?" than which stage of the historical sequence the record resembles, and which gatekeepers are still holding. In Italy and Germany, democracy did not fall at the first emergency decree or the first intimidated election. It fell when the head of state, the army and the courts stopped resisting. The U.S. record from 2017 to 2026 consistently shows those early-sequence methods: emergency stretching, prosecutions of enemies, internal-enemy rhetoric combined with troop deployments, and pressure on elections. It also shows that the later-sequence gatekeepers (courts, grand juries, governors, state election officials, the electorate) have so far blocked the step from erosion to abolition. Even so, the 2024 immunity ruling and the 2026 *Slaughter* decision moved formal power toward the presidency at the same time other rulings checked it. The second term has also weakened two of the skeptics' strongest early arguments, regime weakness and noninterventionism. It has not supplied what the ideal-type historians require: a mass militant party, a revolutionary new order, or the abolition of electoral competition.

For readers preparing to present this material, the most defensible framing is layered. Behavioral frameworks (Levitsky–Ziblatt, Bermeo) register strong and documented matches. Fascism frameworks yield a contested verdict that depends on whether the definition centers behavior (Paxton) or ideology and structure (Griffin, Payne). Quantitative indices place the U.S. in a declining, still-electoral middle category. Several live cases will test which reading the record supports: the November 2026 midterms, the Fourth Circuit's decision on the Comey and James appeal, *Disney/ABC v. FCC*, the Harvard appeal, and the D.C. Guard deployment. If the midterms run freely and their results are accepted, the competitive-authoritarian and patrimonial readings gain ground. Federal interference in the vote would move the record toward the step that, in every interwar case, marked the point of no return.
